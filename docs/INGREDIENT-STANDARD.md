# Robert's Recipe Box Ingredient Standard

Version 1.14 — Loaf & Bread category finalized September 5, 2026

Each audited ingredient separates: canonical inventory identity, recipe-facing
name, cooking quantity and unit, preparation, and practical shopping equivalent.

## Core rules

- Canonical identity describes the food, not capitalization, preparation, package, or measurement.
- Recipe-approved alternatives remain separate identities in one choice group; either may satisfy the recipe.
- Cooking units are singular internally and pluralized only for display.
- Meat, poultry, and seafood primarily use pounds or ounces.
- Chopped, diced, and sliced produce generally use cups; naturally whole produce may use `each`.
- Onion equivalents are: small = 1/2 cup, medium = 1 cup, large = 1 1/2 cups, extra-large = 2 cups.
- Package sizes never live inside the cooking unit. A 15-ounce can is 15 ounces needed plus a one-can shopping equivalent.
- Preparation such as sliced, diced, drained, softened, divided, or optional is stored separately.
- `optional`, `to taste`, and `as needed` are instructions, never units.
- Uncertain conversions are retained from the source and marked `needs-review`; they are never guessed.

## Hamburger category additions

- HB-001–HB-031 and HBP-001–HBP-012 use the five-part ingredient model.
- Ground beef 80/20 and 90/10 remain distinct canonical inventory variants under the Ground Beef family.
- Burger buns remain whole-item counts, with the recipe-approved bun variety retained.
- Bacon strips are standardized as recipe-facing slices and use approximate raw-bacon ounce equivalents for shopping.
- Whole tomatoes, avocados, eggs, and buns remain `each`; prepared produce and toppings retain cup measures.
- Sized prepared onions follow the approved cup equivalents, including 2 large onions = 3 cups and 2 small onions = 1 cup.
- Unmeasured condiments and per-burger toppings remain visible but are excluded from automatic Shopping Lists.

## Loaf & Bread category additions

- LF-001–LF-015 use the five-part ingredient model; LF-FZ1 and LF-FZ2 are ingredient-free reference guides.
- US cooking measures are standardized while printed gram and milliliter equivalents remain visible in recipe text.
- Yeast retains its teaspoon cooking quantity and one-packet practical shopping equivalent.
- Unequal fresh/dried substitutions retain both approved quantities instead of forcing an inaccurate conversion.
- Temperature, dough/topping purpose, dusting, egg-wash, and other preparation instructions remain separate from identity.
- Unmeasured pretzel salt and measured optional ingredients remain visible but are excluded from automatic Shopping Lists.

## Approved missing-detail defaults

- Optional garnishes without amounts remain visible in recipes and are excluded from automatic Shopping Lists.
- Six cube steaks or six chicken cutlets without total weight retain the six-piece recipe amount and use about 2 pounds for inventory and shopping.
- An unspecified prepared onion means 1 cup, with about 1 medium onion as the shopping equivalent.
- A displayed 3–4-pound ham range uses 3 1/2 pounds only for consolidation, cost, and inventory calculations.
- An unspecified diced potato in a measured filling means 1 cup, with about 1 medium russet potato as the shopping equivalent.
- Two cups cooked diced or shredded chicken use about 1 pound raw boneless chicken for shopping; 3 cups use about 1 1/2 pounds.
- Six cups mashed potatoes use about 3 pounds potatoes for shopping; prepared mashed potatoes are an accepted alternative.
- Unmeasured serving suggestions remain visible in recipes and are excluded from automatic Shopping Lists.
- Six cooked bacon slices use about 6 ounces raw bacon for shopping; 1/2 cup cooked crumbled bacon uses about 8 ounces raw.
- Six mixed bone-in chicken pieces remain six recipe pieces and use about 3 pounds for shopping.
- The same approved chicken yield extends proportionally: 4 cups cooked chicken use about 2 pounds raw boneless chicken.
- Six bone-in chicken thighs use about 3 pounds for shopping.
- Unmeasured optional accompaniments and optional ingredients remain visible in recipes and are excluded from automatic Shopping Lists.
- Six boneless pork chops use about 2 pounds for shopping; six bone-in pork chops use about 3 pounds.
- Six half-inch ham steaks use about 3 pounds for shopping.
- One meaty ham hock uses about 1 1/2 pounds for shopping; the recipe-approved alternative of 2 cups diced ham uses about 12 ounces.
- Four cups prepared mashed potatoes use about 2 pounds potatoes or prepared mashed potatoes for shopping.
- One cup cooked crumbled bacon uses about 16 ounces raw bacon for shopping.
- Two cups cooked diced ham use about 12 ounces diced ham for shopping.
- Sized red, white, yellow, and sweet onions use the same approved volume equivalents as standard onions while retaining the onion variety.
- Two cups sliced cooked turkey use about 1 pound raw boneless turkey or 12 ounces prepared cooked turkey for shopping.
- Package-size separation applies to bottles and bags as well as cans and jars.
- Ingredient preparation written in the source unit, such as `drained`, is moved into the separate preparation field.
- Repeated ingredients within one recipe may use unit-specific resolutions so a measured ingredient is not confused with an unmeasured frying or serving entry.
- One cup cooked chicken, beef, or pork strips uses about 1/2 pound of the corresponding raw boneless protein for shopping.
- One-half pound cooked shrimp or shredded chicken uses about 3/4 pound raw peeled shrimp or raw boneless chicken; 8 ounces prepared cooked protein is an accepted alternative.
- Italian recipes IT-001–IT-060 use the same canonical identity, cooking-measure, preparation, and Shopping List separation.
- Small onions use 1/2 cup and unspecified medium onions use 1 cup, with whole-onion shopping equivalents retained.
- Approved Italian piece-count conversions supply shopping weights without removing the useful piece counts from recipe instructions.
- Approved prepared-produce conversions use cups for cooking and whole-produce equivalents for shopping.
- Stated can, jar, bag, bottle, and package sizes use ounce-based cooking quantities while retaining practical package information.
- Mexican recipes MX-001–MX-044 use the same canonical identity, cooking-measure, preparation, and Shopping List separation.
- The approved onion and bell-pepper volume rules are reused consistently; stated box sizes are converted to ounces.
- Measured cooked chicken uses its approved raw shopping yield, while unmeasured garnishes, toppings, serving suggestions, and cooking supplies stay off automatic Shopping Lists.
- Two sliced green onions use 1/4 cup for cooking while retaining two whole green onions as the shopping equivalent.
- One cup cooked rice uses about 1/3 cup dry rice for shopping; two cooked cups use about 2/3 cup dry rice.
- Two cups cooked shredded chicken or beef use about 1.5 pounds of the selected raw boneless protein for shopping.
- A tortilla-chip bag without a stated size uses the approved practical default of 10 ounces.
- Seafood recipes SF-001–SF-020 use the same identity, cooking-measure, preparation, and Shopping List separation.
- Approved salmon-fillet, onion, bell-pepper, green-onion, and cooked-rice conversions are reused consistently.
- Hyphenated ounce package descriptions such as `14.75-ounce cans` are normalized to ounces while preserving their practical can equivalents.
- Four tilapia fillets use about 1.5 pounds for shopping, following the approved four-fillet fish standard.
- Four recipe-facing lemon wedges use about one whole lemon as the practical shopping equivalent.
- Quiche & Pies recipes QP-001–QP-030 use the same identity, cooking-measure, preparation, and Shopping List separation.
- Mini pie shells and full-size pie crusts use `each` for cooking while retaining their 5-inch or 9-inch shopping sizes.
- A pinch remains a recognized cooking measure; it is not assigned an invented teaspoon conversion.
- Explicitly optional ingredients remain visible with their measured cooking quantities but are excluded from automatic Shopping Lists.
- Approved cooked bacon, ham, sausage, ground beef, chicken, and crawfish quantities retain their useful recipe measures and use practical raw or prepared shopping weights.
- One-quarter cup fresh basil or 1 tablespoon dried basil remains an approved recipe substitution.
- Two large sliced onions use 3 cups for cooking and about two large onions for shopping.
- Salads & Bowls recipes SB-001–SB-020 use the same identity, cooking-measure, preparation, and Shopping List separation.
- One-half cup cooked chicken uses about 4 ounces raw boneless chicken or 3 ounces prepared cooked chicken for shopping.
- One-half cup cooked ground beef, salmon, or shrimp retains its useful recipe volume and uses the approved raw or prepared shopping yield.
- Measured crab, tuna, Buffalo chicken, and pimento chicken salad retain recipe volumes with practical prepared or canned shopping equivalents.
- Deli meats and deli cheese retain slice counts because slice thickness varies; no unsupported weight conversion is invented.
- Recipe-approved protein, seed, mayonnaise, and pepper alternatives remain explicit substitution groups.
- Measured and unmeasured optional salad additions remain visible but are excluded from automatic Shopping Lists.
- Smoked & Grilled Meats recipes SG-001–SG-005 and SG-008–SG-027 use the same identity, cooking-measure, preparation, and Shopping List separation.
- Recipe-facing weight and count ranges remain visible; their midpoint is used only for consolidation, cost, and inventory calculations.
- Approved chicken breast, leg, quarter, and bone-in thigh counts retain useful piece counts while adding practical shopping weights.
- Two racks baby back ribs retain the rack count and use about 4 pounds for consolidation and shopping.
- Recipe-approved beef, chicken, sauce, acid, oil, butter, and cooking-fat alternatives remain explicit substitution groups.
- Any ingredient explicitly marked optional, whether stated in its name or unit, remains visible but is excluded from automatic Shopping Lists.
- Side Dishes recipes SD-001–SD-052 use the same identity, cooking-measure, preparation, and Shopping List separation.
- Sized onions and prepared bell peppers, zucchini, carrots, and green onions use approved cup equivalents while retaining whole-produce shopping equivalents.
- Whole potatoes, corn, cabbage, and similar produce may retain natural each, ear, or head units.
- Stated cans and bags use ounce-based cooking quantities while preserving practical package equivalents.
- Frozen-food, sweet-potato, and salad-green ranges remain visible; midpoints are used only for consolidation.
- Fresh-or-frozen vegetable choices and other recipe-approved alternatives remain explicit substitution groups.
- Unmeasured cooking supplies, serving choices, and optional additions remain visible but are excluded from automatic Shopping Lists.

## Inventory matching

Inventory reconciliation uses canonical identity first. Exact products satisfy a
requirement automatically. A recipe-approved alternative may also satisfy it and
is labeled as a substitute. Merely related products remain suggestions and do not
automatically clear the Shopping List requirement.

## Shopping consolidation

Shopping requirements consolidate by canonical identity and compatible cooking
unit. Preparation remains visible to the recipe but does not create duplicate
purchases. Practical package suggestions are rounded only on the Shopping List;
recipe quantities are never rounded to store packages.

## Audit checkpoints

Recipes are audited category-by-category. Each checkpoint includes tests, a
production build, an uncertainty report, a complete master ZIP, and a ZIP of only
the files needed for GitHub.
